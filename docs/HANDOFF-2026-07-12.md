## Executive result

Microsoft Defender XDR generated **three custom-detection alerts** from live,
benign endpoint telemetry. Defender correlated all three on the same onboarded
device, `nls-gw-lab`, into incident `628`:

- exact portal title: **Multi-stage incident involving Persistence &
  Exfiltration on one endpoint**;
- severity: **High**;
- state observed: **Active**;
- active alerts: **3/3**;
- service source for all three alerts: **Microsoft Defender for Endpoint**;
- detection source for all three alerts: **Custom detection**;
- automated response actions: **zero**.

The alert records came from Defender evaluation rather than synthetic
`datatable()` rows. The underlying actions were deliberately harmless analogs
that generated the relevant Windows and Defender telemetry.

### Evaluation timing nuance

Microsoft documents that a newly saved custom detection runs immediately and
can inspect up to 30 days of historical data before following its configured
frequency. NLS-GW-LIVE-001 and NLS-GW-LIVE-003 produced alerts during that
initial scheduled evaluation over previously generated benign telemetry.

NLS-GW-LIVE-004 was enabled before a new harmless marker completed at
`12:30:45Z` with `NetworkTransfer=False`. Its alert's first activity predated
rule creation, while its last activity matched that post-creation marker. The
evidence supports the statement that the NRT alert **incorporated a post-rule
event**.
The evidence does not establish that the new marker was the alert's sole cause.

The NLS-GW-005 wizard result is an observed outcome with no assigned root cause.
It is **not** evidence that aggregate custom detections or NLS-GW-005 are
unsupported.

During the validation window, Microsoft custom-detection validation accepted

until that current identifier is tested against the preview provider; the
retained value records provider compatibility and is not presented as the
current canonical ATT&CK taxonomy.

## What the lab proves

- The bounded endpoint actions produced real Microsoft Defender for Endpoint
  telemetry on an onboarded Windows Server 2022 device.
- The exact checked-in queries for NLS-GW-001, NLS-GW-003, NLS-GW-004, and
  NLS-GW-005 matched live telemetry.
- Defender's scheduled and Continuous evaluators created three custom
  alerts from the separately created portal-native validation rules.
- Device entity mapping and same-device incident correlation worked.
- Incident `628` reached High severity with three active alerts out of three.
- No custom rule performed automated isolation, remediation, deletion, or
  containment.
- The Bicep, CI, GitHub OIDC, exact-ID, and evidence-ledger controls work as
  documented.

## What the lab does not prove

- GigaWiper malware, FlockWiper, or another destructive sample was executed.
- A Microsoft-managed GigaWiper malware-family alert was reproduced.
- Real RabbitMQ, Redis, MinIO upload, or other command-and-control traffic ran.
- Files were encrypted, wiped, made unrecoverable, or transferred off-host.
- Recovery or boot configuration was modified.
- The queries detect every GigaWiper variant, obfuscation, living-off-the-land
  alternative, timing change, or evasion technique.
- The native Sentinel Repository connection or a Graph-fallback rule generated
  any of the three attributed alerts.

The safe testing model is comparable to a fire drill: endpoint telemetry,
sensor collection, rule evaluation, alerts, entity mapping, and incident
correlation are real, while the destructive outcome is deliberately absent.

## Deployment-path findings

### Native Sentinel Repository path

- The connection targeted `main` with `CustomDetection` selected.
- The connection identity had the documented prerequisites and could call the
  custom-detection Graph API.
- Native workflow runs failed all six templates with
  `InvalidTemplateDeployment` and inner `ProviderError`.
- The Repository connection visibly reported **Failed**.
- Delegated direct Bicep also reached the provider and returned an internal
  error.
- The unsuccessful result occurred at the native deployment stage after the
  documented prerequisites were met; the root cause remains unconfirmed.
- Preserve the workflow and tracking IDs for Microsoft Support; do not claim a
  confirmed Microsoft defect.

### Manual Graph fallback

- Six exact IDs were created or updated and read back through the bounded
  Graph-only GitHub OIDC workflow.
- During the post-audit validation, the native and Graph revisions were both
  manual and exact-confirmation gated and shared one GitHub concurrency group.
  For public release, the native files were moved to the retained evidence path
  and only the Graph fallback remains active. Its stable lock is preserved for
  a deliberately hardened, freshly generated native workflow; the lock does
  not decide deployment ownership.
- `nls-gw-000-canary` remained disabled; NLS-GW-001 through NLS-GW-005 remained
  enabled.
- Five hourly schedules returned last-run and next-run metadata.
- Every Graph object had zero current or deprecated response actions.
- The six objects did not appear in the unified Detection Rules list during the
  validation window.
- Scheduler metadata is not alert attribution. No alert or incident is claimed
  for a Graph-fallback object.

### Portal-native validation rules

The three `NLS-GW-LIVE-*` rules are separate portal-created validation objects.
Their alerts must not be attributed to either the failed Repository path or the
six Graph-fallback IDs.

## Editorial language guardrails

Use these formulations in future edits:

- **Say:** “Defender generated three custom alerts from live, benign endpoint
  telemetry.”
- **Do not say:** “GigaWiper was executed” or “the alerts prove live malware.”
- **Say:** “NLS-GW-LIVE-001 and NLS-GW-LIVE-003 alerted during the initial
  historical evaluation.”
- **Say:** “The NLS-GW-LIVE-004 NRT alert incorporated the post-rule marker as
  its last activity.”
- **Do not say:** “The marker was proven to be the sole cause of the NRT alert.”
- **Say:** “NLS-GW-005 matched live telemetry, but no portal rule or alert is
  claimed after the observed entity-loading result.”
- **Do not say:** “Aggregation is unsupported.”
- **Say:** “The native Repository path failed with an unconfirmed root cause.”
- **Do not say:** “Native Repository deployment succeeded” or “Microsoft
  confirmed a provider defect.”
- **Say:** “Graph scheduler metadata was observed.”
- **Do not attribute** the three portal alerts to the six Graph-fallback rules.
